Who gets blurred, how fast, on which GPU

Every pipeline tried for blurring women in street video, split into its four jobs: find people (detector), cut them out pixel-exactly (segmentation mask), keep who-is-who (tracker) and decide woman or not (classifier). Each is timed and scored on the same clip.

clip ali-dawah street interview · 300 frames · 1280×720 · 25 fps · 12 s · ~60 people GPUs NVIDIA L4 22 GB (Ada) for the pipeline comparison, RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell 96 GB for the fast pipeline · torch 2.8.0+cu128 ground truth 21 hand-labelled people (11 women, 10 men)
Verdict

What the numbers say

93 Hz

On an RTX PRO 6000, the fast RF-DETR + McByte + CLIP pipeline goes from video in to blurred video out at 85–93 Hz (the 12 s clip in 3.5 s), or 130 Hz with XLarge and 5 crops per person (accuracy not yet checked). Scored on the L4, it blurs 70% of women's frames, with 2.9% false blur on men and no woman missed entirely.

80% / 21%

SAM 3 tracked still blurs the most of women's frames (80%), but it also blurs men in 21% of their frames, because "woman" and "man" masks land on the same person 45% of the time.

3.9 Hz

is the ceiling of any SAM 3.1-based pipeline on an L4. Its detector alone costs 260 ms per frame, and 194 ms of that is the ViT backbone; its own tracker adds about 177 ms on top. RF-DETR-Seg 2XLarge does the same detect-and-mask job in 44 ms.

= oracle

At a threshold of 0.25, the per-track CLIP vote scored the same as the correct hand labels on this clip, so the remaining gap is detection and tracking coverage, not gender. (That threshold was tuned on the same 21 people.)

The fast pipeline: speed on one big GPU

tracking/fast_blur.py runs RF-DETR-Seg + McByte + CLIP in one process: decode, detect, track, classify, blur and encode. It skips RF-DETR's full-resolution mask output and keeps masks on the GPU; frames are batched 8 at a time. Rates cover all of that; model loading (6.4–7 s) is listed separately. The 12 s clip is 300 frames and the 48 s video is 1,200.

SAM uses its text prompt as the classifier, so one model does several jobs. The two-stage pipelines give each job its own component, which is what lets the jobs be timed and swapped separately.

Next

Where to spend effort

  • Detection coverage, not gender. With correct hand labels forced in, RF-DETR + McByte still reaches only 0.70 of women's frames against SAM 3's 0.80. The loss is frames where a woman's track is split, not yet started, or her mask doesn't cover enough of her. Try lowering McByte's new-track threshold (0.5 → 0.4), tiling for small background people, and merging split tracks by appearance.
  • Background crowd recall is unmeasured. The hand labels cover mostly foreground people. The label-free check counts 24 of 66 people as never gendered for RF-DETR, but that check requires ≥ 0.5 mask IoU with SAM 3.1's own masks, so it favours SAM. Label a few background people before trusting either number.
  • Re-check the 0.25 threshold on new footage. It was tuned on the same 21 people it is scored on. Every man scored at most 0.22 and the two hardest women 0.26–0.27, so the margin is thin.
  • Keep McByte in box-overlap mode unless a clip shows a gain. Its SAM + Cutie masks cost about 0.3 s per frame here for about 2 points of coverage.
  • Detector speed headroom. RF-DETR XLarge runs at half the cost of 2XLarge (14 vs 28 ms). For SAM, speedups have to come from the backbone (compile, TensorRT, FP8, or detecting every Nth frame) rather than from the heads.
Read with care

Caveats

  • Accuracy comes from one 12-second night-street clip and could move on other footage. Detector rates carry over to other videos (fixed input size). Tracker and classifier rates depend on how crowded the scene is; see the "Scales with" column.
  • Ground truth: 28 RF-DETR tracks labelled by eye from masked crop strips (confident labels only; 32 ambiguous tracks left out), carried onto 21 people of the SAM 3.1 person layer. The labels are kept locally with the run metrics (not versioned); the scorer is tracking/gender_gt_eval.py.
  • Rates are 1 / (GPU compute per frame), measured one job at a time on the pod. Model loading (5–25 s per model), weight downloads, video decoding and rendering are excluded. The as-run column adds the benchmark's own frame reads and mask encoding.
  • Women blurred is the average, over the 11 hand-labelled women, of the share of their frames where the pipeline's blur covers at least half of the person. Men false-blur is the same measure over the 10 men.
  • SAM 3.1 needed five workarounds for upstream bugs at sam3 commit 2345a4a (memory leak, a crash when a tracked object is removed, session start, an off-by-one on the last frame, and defaults sized for an 80 GB H100). All are verified byte-identical on runs they don't change.
