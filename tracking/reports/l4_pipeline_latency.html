  • Batching people does not help on the L4: 16 people cost the same batched or one by one. The step is GPU compute (attention over a 64×64 feature map per person), not Python or kernel-launch overhead.
  • EdgeTAM memory is bounded (entries flat at ~450–480; 1 prompt frame + last 17 frames per live person). The pipeline around it grows: every decoded frame is kept for the blur pass (~2.7 MB/frame of host RAM) and every mask stays on the GPU until the video ends (~0.5 MB/frame), because the blur waits for the per-person classification.
  • Upstream EdgeTAM crashes when more than one person is batched (.view on an expanded tensor in perceiver.py); the pod copy uses .reshape, which gives identical results.

What 100 Hz means

100 Hz leaves 10 ms per frame for everything. On the L4 today:

  • RF-DETR-Seg 2XL alone takes 25 ms per frame; the PE-Core classifier 16–21 ms; EdgeTAM 218 ms.
  • The McByte pipeline reached 93 Hz (48 s video) on an RTX PRO 6000, where RF-DETR ran about 5× faster than on the L4.
  • For the EdgeTAM pipeline the per-person memory step dominates, so its cost scales with how many people are tracked and how often each is stepped. Making it reach 100 Hz is the next stage.
