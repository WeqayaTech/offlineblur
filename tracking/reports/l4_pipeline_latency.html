Pipeline latency: L4 runs and the H100 production run

Every person-blur pipeline we have run on the L4, from the SAM 3 trackers to the new RF-DETR + EdgeTAM + PE-Core pipeline, measured as milliseconds of compute per video frame and the resulting rate in Hz. A 25 fps video needs 25 Hz to keep up in real time; the stated goal is 100 Hz. The latest pipeline was then run as a production exercise on Modal: one video split over 10 H100 GPUs.

In plain terms

  • What it does: finds every person in a street video, follows each one through the video, decides once per person whether they are a woman, and blurs the women.
  • Quality: the newest version (EdgeTAM tracker) keeps people's outlines and identities clearly better than earlier versions, judged by eye. Not yet scored against hand-checked labels.
  • Speed: on 10 cloud GPUs working in parallel, a 1-minute video is done in about 23 seconds (80 frames per second). One GPU takes about 90 seconds.
  • Cost: about $0.29 per minute of video on 10 GPUs, or $0.11 on one slower GPU. Today's full experiment cost $27, covered by Modal credits.
  • Goal gap: the target is 100 frames per second; we reached 80. The limit is our cloud account's cap of 10 GPUs at once. Raising that cap or one more software speed-up closes the gap.

Pipeline, per chunk (one H100 each)

  1. Decode the chunk's frames plus 32 frames before it, so tracking is already running when the chunk starts.
  2. RF-DETR-Seg 2XL: person boxes and masks (fp16, batch 8, score ≥ 0.15).
  3. EdgeTAM: image encoder once per 8 frames, then one batched memory step for all live people per frame.
  4. Match detections to tracked people (mask IoU ≥ 0.3); start new people from detections ≥ 0.5; drop duplicates (≥ 0.7); end tracks unconfirmed for 4 s.
  5. PE-Core-L/14-336 scores each person's views.
  6. Coordinator: link people across chunk boundaries (mask IoU ≥ 0.5 over the warm-up frames), pool each person's views, vote woman / man (10 views, P(woman)+P(girl) ≥ 0.25).
  7. Each GPU pixelates its women's masks and encodes its frames; the segments are joined without re-encoding.

  • Batching people does not help on the L4: 16 people cost the same batched or one by one, because the L4 is already fully busy. On the H100 it does: one batched step per frame took a single H100 from 4.8 to 16.9 Hz.
  • EdgeTAM memory is bounded (entries flat at ~450–480; 1 prompt frame + last 17 frames per live person). The pipeline around it grows: every decoded frame is kept for the blur pass (~2.7 MB/frame of host RAM) and every mask stays on the GPU until the video ends (~0.5 MB/frame), because the blur waits for the per-person classification.
  • Upstream EdgeTAM crashes when more than one person is batched (.view on an expanded tensor in perceiver.py); the pod copy uses .reshape, which gives identical results.

What 100 Hz means

100 Hz leaves 10 ms per frame for everything.

  • One L4: RF-DETR-Seg 2XL alone takes 25 ms per frame, so the L4 tops out near 40 Hz before any tracking.
  • One H100, EdgeTAM pipeline: about 63 ms per frame (EdgeTAM 46, RF-DETR 5.5, blur 5.3, other ~6): 16–20 Hz.
  • Ten H100s, split in time: 79.4–82.4 Hz measured. More GPUs than the workspace's limit of about 10, or a faster per-GPU EdgeTAM step, is what moves this past 100 Hz.
